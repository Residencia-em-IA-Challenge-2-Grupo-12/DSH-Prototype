# Candidate evidence-admission dossiers

**Status: not approved.** These are research notes for a human admission decision. No entry is active product guidance; no diagnosis, therapy or universal numeric constraints follow from these notes.

The agent has inspected linked primary study abstracts, primary author papers or official guidance as individually stated; it has not independently replicated studies or evaluated full methodology when only an abstract was available. Rights, local relevance and Brazilian Portuguese translation require human review.

Source links and bibliographic dates/DOIs are recorded, but source text snapshots and source-content hashes are not retained. Capture permitted source versions and hashes at admission before claiming a reproducible evidence corpus.

## Admission workflow

For each source, inspect the cited source and passage, confirm version and rights, assess population and context, review any translation, and decide whether a narrowly worded guidance record is appropriate. Keep rejected and modified candidates in an audit trail; activate nothing based solely on this dossier.

## S01-IES-2007 — Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning

- **Status:** Inactive; source and proposed guidance require human approval.
- **Source:** [official evidence-based practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1); published 2007-09.
- **Population and scope:** Main audience is K–12 teachers; guide includes some college evidence and lists postsecondary as relevant. Scheduling learning and retrieval-based review for content-heavy subjects.
- **Evidence assessment:** Guide rates spacing moderate and re-exposure through quizzes strong; grades are specific to each recommendation, not the whole guide.
- **Limits:** The authors primarily target grades 3–12 and teachers; grade-specific findings do not directly establish benefits for adult scheduling assistants.
- **Rights and language:** The report states it is in the public domain and permission to reprint is unnecessary. Verify any separately owned embedded material and translation-specific obligations before reuse. No approved PT-BR translation exists in this dossier.
- **P01-SPACING — source location:** Recommendation 1, page 5 of full guide; https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/20072004.pdf Paraphrase: The panel recommends re-exposure spaced over time for material not naturally revisited and rates the evidence moderate.
- **P02-RETRIEVAL — source location:** Recommendation 5b, printed page 21 (PDF page 32); evidence summary on printed page 2; https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/20072004.pdf Paraphrase: The panel rates quiz-based re-exposure to key content strong within its guide’s framework and recommends corrective feedback.
- **Candidate product application — inference, not an approved rule:** When a student values retention and has time, offer spaced review or retrieval as editable options grounded in the relevant course.
- **Misuse to avoid:** Do not apply the guide’s multi-week spacing as a mandatory minimum or claim every student and subject benefits equally.

## S02-CEPEDA-2008 — Spacing effects in learning: a temporal ridgeline of optimal retention

- **Status:** Inactive; source and proposed guidance require human approval.
- **Source:** [primary learning experiment; abstract inspected](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/); published 2008-11; DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x.
- **Population and scope:** More than 1,350 participants learned a set of facts; PubMed indexes adolescents through older adults. Gap between initial learning and one review relative to a later factual-recall test.
- **Evidence assessment:** Single controlled study; evidence of timing interaction within this task, not a systematic evidence rating.
- **Limits:** Factual recall, chosen review/test intervals and the experiment’s specific procedures do not define best spacing for full courses or personal wellbeing; methods/full text still need review.
- **Rights and language:** Reuse of publication content and any translation is not cleared; link and paraphrase only. No approved PT-BR translation exists in this dossier.
- **P03-TIMING — source location:** PubMed abstract, Methods/Results; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/#abstract Paraphrase: Longer test delays were associated with different study-review gaps in the studied fact-learning task.
- **Candidate product application — inference, not an approved rule:** Consider a student’s actual deadline and expected review opportunities when proposing spacing; surface uncertainty.
- **Misuse to avoid:** Do not convert the experimental gap percentages into a universal scheduling formula or precise individual guarantee.

## S03-KARPICKE-2008 — The critical importance of retrieval for learning

- **Status:** Inactive; source and proposed guidance require human approval.
- **Source:** [primary learning experiment; abstract inspected](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/); published 2008-02; DOI 10.1126/science.1152408.
- **Population and scope:** University students learning foreign-language vocabulary. Repeated recall testing versus additional study after an item was first recalled.
- **Evidence assessment:** Controlled single experiment; no general evidence grade independently assigned.
- **Limits:** Specific vocabulary and delayed-recall setting; the abstract does not establish effects for all coursework, assessment formats, accessibility needs or schedule duration.
- **Rights and language:** Article content/reuse and translations not cleared; cite link and paraphrase. No approved PT-BR translation exists in this dossier.
- **P04-RETRIEVAL — source location:** PubMed abstract; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/#abstract Paraphrase: Repeated testing after initial recall improved delayed recall in the study’s vocabulary task.
- **Candidate product application — inference, not an approved rule:** Offer voluntary practice questions or recall prompts when suitable materials exist.
- **Misuse to avoid:** Do not equate an optional practice block with a guaranteed grade improvement or require testing for every study session.

## S04-BAKER-2019 — Does Inducing Students to Schedule Lecture Watching in Online Classes Improve Their Academic Performance?

- **Status:** Inactive; source and proposed guidance require human approval.
- **Source:** [primary randomized trial; full author-hosted paper inspected](https://www.rachelbbaker.com/s/Baker-Evans-Li-Cung-2018-Time-Management-RiHE.pdf); published 2019-06; DOI 10.1007/s11162-018-9521-3.
- **Population and scope:** 157 students were randomly assigned in one for-credit online course at a US public university. The main analytic sample contained 145 pre-course survey completers; full-sample analyses included all 157 randomized students, and demographic-covariate analyses used 111. Two early-week email/survey prompts to schedule video watching vs control surveys.
- **Evidence assessment:** Randomized single-course trial; initial quiz effect did not persist to overall course score.
- **Limits:** Specific low-cost video-watching intervention, one institution and short course; not a test of a collaborative calendar assistant, individualized constraints or wellbeing. The WWC review assessed the final course-grade outcome as indeterminate, without a statistically significant positive finding; it did not establish that the paper’s first-quiz result was absent. Review: https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Study/89702
- **Rights and language:** Springer copyright appears in author-hosted PDF; redistribution/extraction/translation permissions not established, use links and paraphrases pending review. No approved PT-BR translation exists in this dossier.
- **P05-MIXED-SCHEDULING — source location:** PDF abstract, journal page 521 (PDF first page), and Experimental Design, journal page 533; https://www.rachelbbaker.com/s/Baker-Evans-Li-Cung-2018-Time-Management-RiHE.pdf Paraphrase: The authors reported a positive first-quiz outcome, no overall course-score difference, and no evidence of changes in measured procrastination, cramming or the timing of work. Clickstream records did not measure total time engaged.
- **Candidate product application — inference, not an approved rule:** Treat potential benefits of scheduling as hypotheses to evaluate and allow adaptation or refusal.
- **Misuse to avoid:** Do not claim calendar blocks cause better grades, lower procrastination or lasting behavior change.

## S05-CLARK-2021 — Mental contrasting with implementation intentions increases study time for university students

- **Status:** Inactive; source and proposed guidance require human approval.
- **Source:** [primary randomized trial; abstract inspected](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33315247/); published 2021-09; DOI 10.1111/bjep.12396.
- **Population and scope:** 58 students at one Australian university. Tailored goal-attainment and MCII training versus stress-management training, four-week follow-up.
- **Evidence assessment:** Small randomized study; reported differences in tailored goal-attainment measures.
- **Limits:** Intervention combines specific methods and increased study time was its chosen goal; abstract-only review cannot establish applicability to an agent, grades or quality of life.
- **Rights and language:** Publisher rights and translation/reuse not established; link and paraphrase only. No approved PT-BR translation exists in this dossier.
- **P06-GOAL-TAILORING — source location:** PubMed abstract Methods/Results; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33315247/#abstract Paraphrase: This small study used individually tailored academic goals and found a difference between the tested interventions in goal-attainment measures.
- **Candidate product application — inference, not an approved rule:** Ask users about their own goals and practical obstacles when helpful; offer, rather than impose, a structured planning exercise.
- **Misuse to avoid:** Do not infer that more study hours is inherently desirable, claim therapy or guarantee wellbeing gains.

## S06-WHOQOL — WHOQOL: Measuring Quality of Life

- **Status:** Inactive; source and proposed guidance require human approval.
- **Source:** [official WHO conceptual and instrument information](https://www.who.int/tools/whoqol); published undated web page; featured instrument publication 2012-03-01.
- **Population and scope:** Cross-cultural quality-of-life conceptual framework; tools were developed with 15 international field centres. Subjective quality of life in relation to the individual’s goals, concerns, culture and environment.
- **Evidence assessment:** Authoritative conceptual framework and instrument background, not an intervention effect estimate.
- **Limits:** A calendar, productivity score or text sentiment does not measure WHOQOL; instrument scoring, licensed administration and translation need separate decisions.
- **Rights and language:** WHO notes linked translations were not created by WHO and may differ; review translation, instrument use and licensing before reuse. No approved PT-BR translation exists in this dossier.
- **P07-SUBJECTIVE-QOL — source location:** WHOQOL page, definition and Introducing the instruments; https://www.who.int/tools/whoqol Paraphrase: WHO describes quality of life as an individual’s perception within their values, environment, goals and concerns.
- **Candidate product application — inference, not an approved rule:** Let students describe what matters and correct inferred preferences; do not substitute scheduled hours for wellbeing.
- **Misuse to avoid:** Do not generate a quality-of-life score or diagnostic conclusion from calendar data.

## S07-EMA-2024 — Investigating Best Practices for Ecological Momentary Assessment: Nationwide Factorial Experiment

- **Status:** Inactive; source and proposed guidance require human approval.
- **Source:** [primary randomized factorial trial; full text inspected](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11347889/); published 2024-08-12; DOI 10.2196/50275.
- **Population and scope:** 411 analyzed US adults, mean age 48.4; majority women and White; participants were compensated. 28-day smartphone self-report protocol, manipulating prompt frequency, item count, timing, payment and response scales.
- **Evidence assessment:** Randomized multi-factor trial; no statistically significant main effects/interactions of tested design factors on completion.
- **Limits:** Paid research data collection is not a study-planning product; non-significance is not proof of equivalence. 69 participants with unusual prompt-delivery issues and five nonresponders were excluded.
- **Rights and language:** Article reports CC BY 4.0; retain attribution and verify exact license/translation obligations at admission. No approved PT-BR translation exists in this dossier.
- **P08-TRACKING-BURDEN — source location:** Abstract Methods/Results and Study Strengths and Limitations; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11347889/ Paraphrase: The tested differences in prompting and survey design did not show significant main effects on completion in this compensated 28-day trial.
- **Candidate product application — inference, not an approved rule:** Make feedback optional and adjustable; evaluate burden and missed data in this product rather than assuming a universal cadence.
- **Misuse to avoid:** Do not prescribe two or four prompts per day, or claim fewer prompts necessarily improve completion.

## S09-NIOSH-SLEEP — NIOSH Training for Nurses on Shift Work and Long Work Hours: How Much Sleep Do You Need?

- **Status:** Inactive; source and proposed guidance require human approval.
- **Source:** [official NIOSH educational guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/work-hour-training-for-nurses/longhours/mod2/08.html); published last reviewed 2020-03-31.
- **Population and scope:** Adult workers, with a specific training focus on nurses and shift work. Sleep adequacy and individual variation in an occupational education context.
- **Evidence assessment:** Educational guidance cites underlying evidence; no intervention effect grade assigned here.
- **Limits:** Person-to-person sleep patterns, shift schedules and circumstances vary; no calendar record can establish actual sleep quality or duration.
- **Rights and language:** Government web content and referenced materials require reuse/translation review before ingestion. No approved PT-BR translation exists in this dossier.
- **P10-REST-VARIATION — source location:** Module 2, page 8, How Much Sleep Do You Need?; https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/work-hour-training-for-nurses/longhours/mod2/08.html Paraphrase: NIOSH notes individual variation in sleep needs and discusses sleep duration and quality.
- **Candidate product application — inference, not an approved rule:** Ask about stated sleep or rest needs when relevant and avoid assigning study into user-protected rest.
- **Misuse to avoid:** Do not infer actual sleep from a calendar, impose one universal bedtime, diagnose sleep disorders or recommend a clinical treatment.

## Cross-source design implications to review

- Treat the person’s expressed goals, constraints and corrections as the primary source of their preferences. Neither evidence about a population nor a calendar gap establishes an individual preference.
- When relevant and feasible, offer optional review or retrieval opportunities with provenance and flexible timing; the evidence does not specify one mandatory schedule for every person.
- Report the randomized scheduling trial’s mixed outcomes alongside positive learning-strategy evidence. A proposed plan, assigned hours and self-reported activity are distinct observations; none independently proves improved wellbeing.
- Avoid creating clinical recommendations from general health guidance. Any potentially consequential health claim, adapted population guidance or translated wording needs qualified review.
- Ask optional questions or check-ins when useful, and evaluate actual burden in this application. The EMA trial does not establish an optimal cadence for this product.
